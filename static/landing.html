How a story works

Each story is six short parts. Mr. Sprout reads one, then it's your child's turn to talk.

  1. 1

    Mr. Sprout reads

    A warm narrator voice reads each part of the story, with pictures and gentle sound effects.

  2. 2

    Your child answers

    They say the practice word out loud, or answer an open question in their own words.

  3. 3

    Mr. Sprout cheers them on

    Specific, kind feedback after every answer. A missed word gets up to three friendly tries, never a scolding.

Powered by ElevenLabs: text-to-speech gives Mr. Sprout a voice, speech-to-text lets Mr. Sprout hear your child’s answers, and every sound effect in the stories is made with ElevenLabs too!

For parents and speech therapists

Kids have fun. You see how practice is going.

⭐

Progress stars

Every finished story earns stars, and a history of past sessions shows how practice is going over time.

📝

Plain-language reports

A practice summary with real numbers, like practice-word accuracy and what changed since the first session sent over to parent's email, ready to share with your therapist.

🎙️

Voice insights

Speaking pace, pauses and pitch variation are measured from each recording and shown as things to talk over with the therapist, not as a diagnosis.

StoryBuddy supports speech-therapy practice. It does not diagnose any condition and does not replace a speech therapist.

Backed by research

StoryBuddy is built on speech-therapy research, including research on apps for kids with autism.

  • Speech games at home work. In a clinical trial, children aged 4–10 who played a speech game at home for four weeks spoke more accurately. The study also tracked voice measures, as Mr. Sprout does. Kim et al., 2023 · JMIR Serious Games
  • Designed with autistic kids in mind. A review of 43 studies of autism apps found that child-centred design and game-like play keep kids engaged. Liu et al., 2023 · Sensors
  • Progress notes help practice stick. Speech therapists recommend short, positive progress notes to carry practice over at home. StoryBuddy’s stars and reports do just that. Speech Therapy Fun blog, 2026
